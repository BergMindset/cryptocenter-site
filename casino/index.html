Strategy & game theory

Know the maths before you play

Short, worked explanations. None of them make the house edge disappear — they show you where it sits.

2.7%Expected value: the house edge in one numberRoulette · 1 min

European roulette has 37 pockets but pays 35 to 1 on a single number. That gap is the house edge: 2.7%. Over a thousand $1 spins you should expect to be about $27 down. Short sessions swing both ways; long ones drift towards that number.

96%RTP is not what you'll get back tonightSlots · 1 min

A 96% RTP describes millions of spins, not your evening. Volatility decides how the missing 4% is taken: low-volatility slots nibble with frequent small wins, high-volatility slots go quiet for long stretches and then pay big. Same RTP, completely different ride.

35×Wagering requirements, worked outBonuses · 1 min

A $100 bonus at 35× means $3,500 of bets before you can cash out. On a slot with a 4% house edge, that much betting costs about $140 on average — more than the bonus is worth. Do the multiplication before you accept.

$1,280Why the Martingale system breaksBetting systems · 2 min

Double your bet after every loss and one win recovers everything — until a losing streak arrives. Start at $10 and seven losses in a row make your eighth bet $1,280, with $1,270 already gone. The system doesn't change the house edge; it swaps many small wins for one rare, very large loss.

0.5%Basic strategy in blackjackBlackjack · 1 min

Basic strategy is the mathematically best decision for every hand against every dealer card. Followed exactly, it brings the house edge down to around 0.5% under common rules. Playing hunches instead of the chart is the most expensive habit at the table.

LimitBankroll: decide the loss before you startBankroll · 1 min

Pick the amount you can afford to lose, split it into sessions, and end a session when its share is gone. Chasing losses — raising stakes to win it back — is the pattern help services see more than any other.
